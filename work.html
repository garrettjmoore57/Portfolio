01 / Brand & messaging

Make every touchpoint
sound like the same business.

A brand is not just a logo. It is how the business shows up in a sales deck, on a brochure, on a website and in the words people choose.

01

One documented brand refresh, supported by a new look and written guidelines.

Scope, not a measured business outcome.

The challenge

Prospective buyers meet the business in several places. Without shared direction, those pieces can send different signals.

My role

I led a refresh of the company brand, with a new look and written guidelines. I worked on the strategy, direction and resources that carried the identity into sales conversations. I also developed the voice and messaging for social posts.

What I worked on

  • A visual direction the business could use across materials.
  • Written guidance so future work starts from shared rules.
  • Messaging for social posts, print and presentations.

Why it matters

The team has a consistent starting point when it needs to explain the business. This is about making future decisions clearer; I am not claiming a measured sales impact.

Actual before/after identity examples. I led the brand refresh but do not claim sole authorship of the logo artwork or a measured sales result.

02 / Sales support

Help the sales team
explain the offer.

03 / Campaign planning

Make follow-up
a plan, not a guess.

When outreach is shared across a sales team, the next step should be clear to the person making the call.

181

School district superintendents and principals included in the campaign plan.

Three emails and two calls. This is planned reach, not a response or conversion total.

The challenge

Sales outreach needs a repeatable rhythm. Without a clear sequence, every rep may have to decide what to send and when to follow up.

My role

I built a campaign for 181 school district superintendents and principals using a three-email, two-call sequence designed for reps to reuse.

What I built

  • A five-touch outreach structure.
  • Email messaging and follow-up steps.
  • A reusable plan for the sales team.

Why it matters

A shared sequence makes the next step easier to follow. I don't have verified open, reply, meeting or revenue figures to attach to this example.

The outreach diagram illustrates the documented cadence. It is not a screenshot of a campaign platform or a report of completed outreach.

04 / Competitive strategy

When price isn't your edge,
give buyers another reason.

A new entrant came in at roughly 30% lower prices. The useful response was a better service story, not an invented price advantage.

30%

Approximate price gap presented by a new competitor.

Context for the challenge, not a measure of sales won or prices changed.

The challenge

When a competitor is substantially cheaper, a sales team needs to explain what else matters to the buyer.

My role

I built a partnership offer and a map of how Texas school districts buy. The work supported a response based on service and relationships.

What I built

  • A partnership offer for sales to discuss.
  • A view of the buying process to inform positioning.
  • A way to frame the conversation around service, not only price.

Why it matters

The team has a clearer story to bring to the buyer. This describes the strategy and deliverables, not a claimed win rate or financial result.

The route diagram on the homepage is conceptual, not a real customer map or an export of internal purchasing information.

05 / Field photography

Sometimes the finished space
is the best sales material.

I photographed two completed school furniture environments. These images make it easier to show what a delivered project looks like, beyond a rendering or product cutout.

The challenge

Buyers want to see real environments, not just furniture catalog pages.

My role

I visited the projects and took these photographs for marketing use. The furnishings and space design were completed by the company's project team; my credited contribution here is photography and marketing presentation.

What I made

  • Photographs documenting an open career and technical education space.
  • Photographs of a college-preparation learning commons.
  • Visual material for future collateral, sales presentations and project showcases.

Why it matters

Genuine project images give sales teams a more useful way to show what the organization delivers. I am not claiming a measurable sales increase from these photographs.

The second image is cropped to exclude school-identifying signage. No district names or project performance results are published.
